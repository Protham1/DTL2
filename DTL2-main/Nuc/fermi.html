Enrico Fermi's Nuclear Chain Reaction

Overview

In 1942, Italian physicist Enrico Fermi led the world's first controlled nuclear chain reaction at the University of Chicago. This historic achievement was part of the Manhattan Project and marked the beginning of the nuclear age.

The experiment, conducted in a makeshift laboratory under the stands of Stagg Field, demonstrated that a self-sustaining nuclear reaction was possible. Fermi and his team used a graphite and uranium reactor, known as Chicago Pile-1, to achieve this feat.

Impact

Fermi's nuclear chain reaction experiment paved the way for the development of nuclear reactors and nuclear power. It also had significant military implications, contributing to the creation of atomic bombs during World War II.
