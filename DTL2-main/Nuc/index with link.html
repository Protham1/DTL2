Timeline of Nuclear Physics

Explore key discoveries and events in the field of nuclear physics.

Henri Becquerel Discovers Radioactivity

In 1896, Henri Becquerel discovered radioactivity while studying uranium salts, laying the foundation for modern nuclear physics and atomic research.

1896

Marie and Pierre Curie Isolate Radium

In 1898, the Curies isolated radium and polonium, significantly advancing the study of radioactive materials and earning them global recognition.

1898

Ernest Rutherford's Gold Foil Experiment

In 1909, Rutherford's experiment revealed the nucleus, disproving the plum pudding model and introducing the nuclear model of the atom.

1909

Discovery of the Proton by Rutherford

In 1917, Rutherford identified the proton as a fundamental particle within the nucleus, deepening our understanding of atomic structure.

1917

Discovery of the Neutron by James Chadwick

In 1932, James Chadwick discovered the neutron, a neutral particle within the nucleus, completing the basic model of atomic structure.

1932

Enrico Fermi's Nuclear Chain Reaction

In 1942, Fermi achieved the first controlled nuclear chain reaction, marking the start of nuclear energy's practical applications.

1942

Development of the Atomic Bomb (Manhattan Project)

The Manhattan Project (1942–1945) led to the creation of the atomic bomb, fundamentally altering the course of World War II and history.

1945

First Experimental Nuclear Reactor (Chicago Pile-1)

Chicago Pile-1 was the first artificial nuclear reactor, built in 1942, enabling the study of controlled fission reactions.

1942

Discovery of Nuclear Fission

In 1938, Otto Hahn and Fritz Strassmann discovered nuclear fission, leading to groundbreaking developments in energy and weaponry.

1938

First Controlled Thermonuclear Fusion

In 1952, scientists achieved controlled thermonuclear fusion, a potential future source of sustainable and clean energy.

1952
