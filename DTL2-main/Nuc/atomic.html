Development of the Atomic Bomb (Manhattan Project)

Overview

The Manhattan Project was a secret U.S. government research project during World War II that led to the development of the first nuclear weapons. It began in 1939 and culminated in 1945 with the successful detonation of atomic bombs in Hiroshima and Nagasaki, Japan.

The project was led by physicist J. Robert Oppenheimer and involved scientists from multiple countries. The research focused on nuclear fission reactions and the development of uranium-235 and plutonium-239 bombs. The project reached a turning point with the successful test of the first atomic bomb, code-named "Trinity," in July 1945.

Impact

The Manhattan Project fundamentally changed global warfare and international relations. It introduced the world to the destructive power of nuclear weapons and led to the establishment of nuclear policies that continue to influence global politics today.
