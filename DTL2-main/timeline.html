Timeline of the Mughal Empire

Explore key events and figures in the history of the Mughal Empire.

Founding of the Mughal Empire

1526

Babur, a descendant of Timur and Genghis Khan, defeats Ibrahim Lodi at the First Battle of Panipat, establishing the Mughal Empire in India.

Humayun's Reign and Exile

1530-1556

Humayun, Babur's son, faces challenges and is briefly exiled by Sher Shah Suri. He eventually reclaims the throne but his reign is marked by instability.

Akbar's Reign and Consolidation

1556-1605

Akbar, one of the greatest Mughal emperors, expands the empire significantly, implements religious tolerance policies, and establishes a centralized administration.

Jahangir's Reign and Cultural Flourishing

1605-1627

Jahangir's reign is known for its artistic and cultural achievements, including the patronage of painting and architecture. He also continues the expansion of the empire.

Shah Jahan's Reign and Architectural Marvels

1628-1658

Shah Jahan commissions the construction of the Taj Mahal and other iconic monuments. His reign is considered a golden age of Mughal architecture but is also marked by political intrigues.

Aurangzeb's Reign and Expansion, Religious Policies

1658-1707

Aurangzeb's long reign sees the Mughal Empire reach its greatest territorial extent. However, his strict religious policies and wars contribute to the empire's decline in later years.

Decline of the Mughal Empire

1707-1857

Following Aurangzeb's death, the Mughal Empire gradually weakens due to internal strife, regional rebellions, and the rise of the Maratha Empire and the British East India Company.

The Sepoy Mutiny of 1857

1857

The Sepoy Mutiny, though ultimately unsuccessful, marks a significant turning point. The British Crown assumes direct control of India, formally ending the Mughal Empire's rule.

Bahadur Shah Zafar II's Exile

1858

Bahadur Shah Zafar II, the last Mughal emperor, is exiled to Rangoon, Burma (present-day Myanmar) by the British, marking the final chapter of the Mughal dynasty.

Legacy of the Mughal Empire

Post 1857

The Mughal Empire left a lasting impact on India's culture, art, architecture, and political landscape. Its influence is still visible today in various aspects of Indian society.
