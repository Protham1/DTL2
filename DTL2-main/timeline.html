Timeline of Chemical Kinetics

Explore key discoveries and events in the field of chemical kinetics.

Wilhelmy's Study of Sugar Inversion

1850

Ludwig Wilhelmy studied the acid-catalyzed inversion of sucrose and derived a rate equation showing that the reaction rate was proportional to the concentrations of sucrose and acid. This marked the first quantitative study of reaction rates and is considered the birth of chemical kinetics.

Guldberg and Waage's Law of Mass Action

1864-1879

Cato Maximilian Guldberg and Peter Waage formulated the law of mass action, which states that the rate of a chemical reaction is proportional to the product of the concentrations of the reactants, each raised to a power determined by its stoichiometric coefficient in the balanced chemical equation. They clarified the concepts of reaction equilibrium and reaction rate.

Van't Hoff's Work on Order of Reactions

1884

Jacobus Henricus van 't Hoff published "Études de dynamique chimique," which explored the concept of reaction order and its determination from experimental data. He further categorized reactions as unimolecular, bimolecular, etc. and introduced the influence of temperature on reaction rates. He also introduced the concept of the rate constant.

Arrhenius Equation

1889

Svante Arrhenius proposed the Arrhenius equation, which describes the temperature dependence of the rate constant. It incorporates the activation energy, which represents the energy barrier that reactants must overcome to form products. This equation explained the exponential increase in reaction rates with increasing temperature.
