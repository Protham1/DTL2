Josiah Willard Gibbs and Thermodynamic Potentials

Overview

Josiah Willard Gibbs, an American physicist, and chemist, introduced the concept of thermodynamic potentials, such as Gibbs free energy. His work allowed scientists to predict the direction of chemical reactions and phase transitions under specific conditions.

Gibbs formalized much of thermodynamics in mathematical terms, creating a framework that unified chemical, thermal, and physical processes. His contributions laid the foundation for modern physical chemistry and statistical mechanics.
