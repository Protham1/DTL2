The Third Law of Thermodynamics

Overview

The Third Law of Thermodynamics states that as the temperature of a system approaches absolute zero (\(0 \, \text{K}\)), the entropy of a perfectly crystalline substance also approaches zero. This principle provides a reference point for the calculation of absolute entropies of substances.

Proposed by Walther Nernst in the early 20th century, the law explains that at absolute zero, the molecular motion ceases entirely, and no additional entropy is contributed from random molecular configurations. This law has profound implications in low-temperature physics and chemistry.

Implications

The Third Law of Thermodynamics is fundamental in understanding phenomena like:

  • 1. The behavior of substances near absolute zero.
  • 2. The feasibility of reaching absolute zero in practical systems (it is unattainable).
  • 3. Providing a basis for calculating the absolute entropy of substances.
