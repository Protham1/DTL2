Carnot's Principle

Overview

Sadi Carnot, often referred to as the "father of thermodynamics," introduced the concept of the Carnot cycle in 1824. He analyzed the efficiency of heat engines and proposed that the efficiency of an idealized engine depends solely on the temperature difference between the hot and cold reservoirs, rather than the working substance of the engine.

Carnot Cycle Diagram

This groundbreaking work laid the foundation for the second law of thermodynamics and helped develop the field of energy conservation. Carnot's principles are still applied today in the design of modern engines and refrigerators.
