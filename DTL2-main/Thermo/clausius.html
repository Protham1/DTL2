Rudolf Clausius and Entropy

Overview

Rudolf Clausius, a German physicist, was a pioneer in the field of thermodynamics. In 1850, he introduced the concept of entropy, a measure of disorder or randomness in a system. Clausius redefined the second law of thermodynamics, stating that the entropy of an isolated system always increases, driving natural processes toward equilibrium.

Clausius's work laid the foundation for understanding heat transfer and energy transformations. His contributions are critical for applications ranging from industrial processes to modern-day physics and engineering.
