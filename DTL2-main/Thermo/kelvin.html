Lord Kelvin and Absolute Temperature

Overview

William Thomson, later known as Lord Kelvin, was a prominent physicist who made significant contributions to thermodynamics. In 1848, he proposed the concept of an absolute temperature scale, now called the Kelvin scale. This scale provides a universal standard for temperature measurement, with absolute zero as its starting point, where all molecular motion ceases.

Kelvin’s work was instrumental in linking temperature to energy and advancing the study of heat engines, paving the way for the second law of thermodynamics. His absolute temperature scale remains a cornerstone of modern physics and engineering.
